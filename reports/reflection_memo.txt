REFLECTION MEMO — XLE Direction Model Research
energy-autoresearch project
Date: 2026-05-27
Author: Autonomous ML Research Agent
===========================================================

WHAT WORKED
-----------

1. Direction classification framing.
   Reframing the problem from regression (predict return magnitude) to classification
   (predict direction, scale by confidence) was the single most impactful architectural
   decision. Because the Sharpe metric rewards only sign correctness, not magnitude accuracy,
   the classification framing aligns the model's training objective more directly with what
   is being evaluated. The performance jump from OLS regression (Sharpe ~0.59) to a
   direction classifier (Sharpe ~0.96) confirmed this immediately.

2. Rolling momentum features.
   The six-feature vector (raw return, vol-adjusted return, 5-day mean, 20-day mean,
   MACD ratio, vol-ratio) captures the relevant signal dimensions without overfitting.
   Attempts to add more features---additional lags, 60-day windows, Bollinger bands,
   streak counters---consistently hurt out-of-sample performance, validating that
   parsimony matters more than breadth when training on ~800 samples.

3. WTI shock filter.
   Going flat after large WTI daily moves added +0.13 to +0.37 Sharpe depending on
   the validation period. This is a clear, falsifiable hypothesis that the momentum
   signal is unreliable on days following commodity regime discontinuities. The mechanism
   is sound: energy ETF returns on the day after a ±2% WTI move are driven by news
   flow rather than momentum, so a pre-trained classifier has no valid edge. Implementing
   this as a post-hoc override rather than an RF input feature was critical---when WTI
   return was added as an input feature, performance dropped sharply (exp7 in Session 2:
   Sharpe 1.34), likely because depth-2 trees cannot decompose the two separate regimes.

4. Long rolling training window (756 days).
   Restricting training to the most recent three years and dropping COVID-era 2020-2021
   data improved Sharpe by over one unit compared to a two-year window. This reflects a
   genuine regime change: post-2022 energy market dynamics (Russia-Ukraine supply disruption,
   Fed rate cycle, ESG-driven sector volatility) differ structurally from the 2020-2021
   pandemic period, which contains distributional outliers that confuse the momentum signal.

5. Random Forest over gradient boosting, SVM, and MLP.
   RF with max_depth=2 and min_samples_leaf=22 was more robust than all tested alternatives.
   Gradient boosting and MLP overfit the small training set. SVM (RBF and linear) produced
   similar results to logistic regression but with higher variance. RF is deterministic
   given a random seed, which makes it operationally reliable across daily data refreshes---
   MLP proved unstable in this regard.


WHAT FAILED
-----------

1. Alternative-data features.
   The src/ data pipeline assembles EIA crude/gas storage, NOAA weather (HDD/CDD),
   GEM pipeline utilisation, and Google Trends signals into a weekly feature matrix.
   Individual signals reached OOS Sharpe of 0.61-1.69 in isolated tests, but none
   improved the daily direction model. The core issue is temporal resolution mismatch:
   EIA and weather data are weekly; the model operates daily. Interpolating weekly
   signals to daily frequency introduces look-ahead bias if not handled carefully, and
   the resulting features are too smooth to add information to the intraweek momentum signal.

2. Direct Sharpe optimization (tanh surrogate).
   Attempts to replace cross-entropy loss with a differentiable Sharpe surrogate (tanh
   of logit scores, optimized via gradient descent on the training Sharpe) produced severe
   overfitting: training Sharpe exceeded 10 while validation Sharpe collapsed to near zero.
   The training objective's landscape is too jagged at this sample size for gradient-based
   optimization to generalize---the model found a solution that perfectly sorted the
   training returns but had no generalization structure.

3. Confidence gating (abstaining on uncertain predictions).
   Applying a threshold on P(up) and abstaining when the prediction was near 0.5 reduced
   Sharpe in all tested configurations. RF class probabilities on financial data cluster
   heavily near 0.5 (the calibration is poor for out-of-distribution days), so gating
   removes most of the signal rather than selecting for high-confidence bets.

4. Sample weighting by return magnitude.
   Upweighting training days with large realized returns (on the hypothesis that
   correctly calling a big move matters more) reduced Sharpe from ~1.40 to ~0.06.
   The lesson: correctly predicting the direction of a small return day is equally
   important for Sharpe accumulation, because the strategy holds the same notional
   position regardless of predicted return magnitude.

5. Walk-forward Sharpe as a primary optimization target.
   Walk-forward Sharpe across four chronological folds ranges from 0.10 to 0.46
   for all models---far lower than the single-split validation Sharpe. This gap
   reflects that the current validation period (2025-2026) is a relatively favorable
   energy momentum regime, while earlier folds are noisier. Attempts to optimize
   hyperparameters against walk-forward Sharpe rather than single-split Sharpe
   produced models that were more "balanced" across regimes but scored lower on
   the primary evaluation.


WHAT I WOULD DO DIFFERENTLY
----------------------------

1. Separate the shock filter from model evaluation more carefully.
   The WTI shock filter overrides predictions after the model is fit; it is not
   evaluated in walk-forward Sharpe (the walk-forward folds use raw model predictions).
   This creates inconsistency in the reported metrics. Future iterations should integrate
   the shock filter logic into a unified estimator class that the walk-forward evaluator
   treats as atomic.

2. Use a proper combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV) scheme.
   The current walk-forward implementation uses simple expanding-window folds without
   purging the test/train boundary. López de Prado (2018) demonstrates that boundary
   overlap introduces information leakage in financial time series. A purged CPCV would
   provide more reliable out-of-sample Sharpe estimates.

3. Treat the training window as a hyperparameter with more structure.
   The sweep tested only 756 vs. 504 days. A more principled approach would identify
   structural breaks in the XLE return series (e.g., using CUSUM or regime-change tests)
   and condition the training window on regime, not calendar time.

4. Invest in the weekly alternative-data pipeline earlier.
   The EIA/weather/GEM signals showed 0.61-1.69 Sharpe in isolation on the weekly
   frequency. A weekly signal ensemble---separate from the daily momentum model---would
   have been worth developing in parallel. The two models could be combined by using
   the weekly directional signal as a position-sizing overlay on the daily momentum signal.

5. Be more systematic about feature importance and partial dependence.
   All experiments used the same six features without checking whether each contributed
   to out-of-sample performance. Feature-level ablation (removing one feature at a time
   and measuring Sharpe impact) would have identified whether any of the six features
   were contributing noise rather than signal.


OPEN QUESTIONS
--------------

1. Is the 2025-2026 validation period genuinely representative of live performance?
   The validation Sharpe is 2.48, but the walk-forward mean is ~0.15-0.46. The gap
   suggests that either (a) the current energy momentum regime is unusually persistent,
   or (b) the single-split evaluation is capturing a specific period of high predictability
   that will revert. Answering this requires extending the validation period as time passes.

2. What is the optimal WTI threshold across market regimes?
   The threshold of 2% was optimized on one validation period. Energy market volatility
   varies substantially across regimes (e.g., 2020 COVID, 2022 Ukraine). A dynamic
   threshold calibrated on a rolling volatility measure (e.g., set threshold at 1.5x
   the rolling 30-day absolute daily WTI return) might be more robust.

3. Can the feature set be extended to include cross-asset signals without overfitting?
   Natural gas futures (NG=F), crack spreads (RBOB-WTI), and XLE option implied
   volatility are all economically motivated candidates. The challenge is that all of
   these are correlated with WTI, so they may add multicollinearity without adding
   independent signal. Principal-component orthogonalization before inclusion warrants
   investigation.

4. Does the model remain predictive intraday?
   All experiments use end-of-day prices. The momentum signal for XLE may have a
   finer time structure that is accessible with intraday bars. The Alpaca API
   integration in src/ was scaffolded but never used for live model evaluation.
